# Findings

All figures below come directly from `outputs/tables/` (generated by
`python run_analysis.py` against the full 6,780-row dataset). Format:
**Finding -> Evidence -> Business Meaning.**

---

### Finding 1: Overall budget utilization is close to, but under, 100%

**Evidence:** Total Budget Allocated = $1,019,783,032.04; Total
Budget Utilized = $978,883,735.36; overall utilization rate = 95.99%.

**Business Meaning:** At the portfolio level, spend tracks closely to
allocation. However, this aggregate figure masks meaningful spread at
the record level (Finding 2), so it should not be read as "every
department is on-budget."

---

### Finding 2: Nearly half of individual records run over their allocation

**Evidence:** 46.9% of the 6,780 records have Budget_Utilized >
Budget_Allocated (`is_overspent`); 30.1% are underspent by more than
30% (`is_underspent`). 258 records (3.8%) show utilization above 3x
allocation.

**Business Meaning:** The near-100% aggregate utilization rate in
Finding 1 is a blend of large overspends and large underspends
roughly offsetting each other - not evidence of consistent budget
discipline. Aggregate KPIs alone would hide this.

---

### Finding 3: Finance shows the highest average over-utilization in specific categories

**Evidence:** The three highest average utilization-rate combinations
(from `top_utilization_combinations.csv`) are Finance x Operations
(130.0%), IT x Salaries (126.1%), and Finance x Training (125.9%).
Finance appears in 3 of the top 6 combinations.

**Business Meaning:** If this were a real organization, Finance's
Operations and Training spend, and IT's Salaries spend, would be the
first places to review for budget overruns.

---

### Finding 4: Department-level allocation efficiency is narrowly clustered

**Evidence:** Average Allocation_Efficiency by department ranges only
from 83.66 (Finance) to 84.32 (Logistics) - a spread of 0.66 points
on a 0-100 scale. Overall average is 84.04.

**Business Meaning:** No department stands out as a clear efficiency
outlier at the aggregate level; differences that matter are more
visible at the Department x Expense_Category grain (Finding 3) than
at the department level alone.

---

### Finding 5: Revenue forecasting is least accurate for Operations and Finance

**Evidence:** Revenue realization rate (Actual / Forecast) by
department: Operations 93.35%, Finance 94.73%, Marketing 94.86%, IT
95.09%, HR 96.09%, Sales 96.13%, R&D 97.35%, Logistics 101.14%
(the only department where actual revenue exceeded forecast).

**Business Meaning:** Operations and Finance under-forecast revenue
accuracy the most; Logistics is the only department whose actual
revenue came in above forecast.

---

### Finding 6: Budget_Status is roughly evenly split, with no department dominating "Inefficient"

**Evidence:** Portfolio-wide, Budget_Status splits Efficient 34.0% /
Inefficient 33.4% / Moderate 32.6%. By department, the "Inefficient"
share ranges narrowly from 32.2% (Finance) to 34.7% (HR) - no
department's inefficient share exceeds 35%.

**Business Meaning:** Budget_Status does not point to any single
department as a systemic problem area; it's evenly distributed,
consistent with Finding 4.

---

### Finding 7: Spending volatility does not meaningfully differ by Budget_Status

**Evidence:** Mean Spending_Volatility by Budget_Status: Inefficient
0.2526, Efficient 0.2509, Moderate 0.2488 - differences of ~0.004,
smaller than the standard deviation (~0.115) within each group.

**Business Meaning:** Spending volatility is not a useful predictor
of whether a record ends up labeled Efficient vs. Inefficient in this
dataset.

---

### Finding 8: No meaningful correlation between inflation, market conditions, and budget outcomes

**Evidence:** Pairwise correlation between Inflation_Rate,
Spending_Volatility, Market_Index, Allocation_Efficiency, and
Budget_Variance_Pct all fall below |r| = 0.03.

**Business Meaning:** In this dataset, macro-style fields
(inflation, market index) show no linear relationship with budget
performance - consistent with the dataset appearing synthetically
generated (see `assumptions_and_constraints.md`, item 6). No causal
or even correlational business story should be built on these
fields.

---

### Finding 9: Fiscal-quarter comparisons show Q2 slightly ahead on efficiency and utilization

**Evidence:** Cross-sectional by quarter label: Q2 has the highest
utilization rate (97.21%) and highest average allocation efficiency
(84.07); Q1 and Q3 are lowest on utilization rate (95.35% each).

**Business Meaning:** Since quarter labels carry no year, this is a
label-level pattern only, not a seasonal trend - it should not be
extrapolated into "budgets improve every Q2."
